Bentonite Test Methods —

Original Text

Bentonite — Test Methods (continued from Section 0a)

Identification — X-ray Diffraction

Sample preparation A: Add 2 g in small portions to 100 mL of water with intense agitation. Allow to stand for 12 h to ensure complete hydration. Place 2 mL of the mixture on a suitable glass slide, and allow to air-dry at room temperature to produce an oriented film. Place the slide in a vacuum desiccator over a free surface of ethylene glycol. Evacuate the desiccator, and close the stopcock so that ethylene glycol saturates the desiccator chamber. Allow the slide to stand for 12 h.

Sample preparation B: Prepare a random powder specimen of the sample.

Analysis: Sample preparation A and Sample preparation B. Record the X-ray diffraction pattern of the samples, and determine the d values.

Identification — Precipitation

Sample: 5 g

Analysis: Add 1 g of potassium nitrate and 3 g of anhydrous sodium carbonate to the Sample in a metal crucible, heat until the mixture has melted, and allow it to cool. Add 20 mL of boiling water to the residue, mix, filter, and wash the residue with 50 mL of water. Add 1 mL of hydrochloric acid and 5 mL of water to the residue, and filter. Add 1 mL of 10 N sodium hydroxide to the filtrate, filter, and add 3 mL of 2 M ammonium chloride.

Arsenic — Sample solution: Transfer 8.0 g of dried sample into a 250-mL beaker containing 100 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid (1 in 25), mix, and cover with a watch glass. Boil gently for 15 min. Pass the hot supernatant liquid through a rapid-flow filter paper into a 200-mL volumetric flask, and wash with four 25-mL portions of hot dilute HCl. Cool, dilute to volume.

Arsenic trioxide stock solution: Accurately weigh 132.0 mg of arsenic trioxide, previously dried at 105° for 1 h, and dissolve in 5 mL of sodium hydroxide solution (1 in 5) in a 1000-mL volumetric flask. Neutralize with 2 N sulfuric acid, add an additional 10 mL of 2 N sulfuric acid, and bring to volume.

Standard arsenic solution: Dilute the stock solution to suitable concentrations within the linear working range of the instrument. Keep in all-glass container, use within 3 days.

Analysis: Proceed according to Elemental Impurities—Procedures 〈233〉.

Lead — Sample solution: Transfer 3.75 g of sample into 250-mL beaker containing 100 mL of dilute HCl (1 in 25), boil for 15 min, cool, pass through filter paper into 400-mL beaker. Concentrate to ~20 mL; if precipitate forms, add 2–3 drops nitric acid. Pass through filter into 50-mL volumetric flask, dilute to volume.

Lead nitrate stock solution: Dissolve 159.8 mg of lead nitrate in 100 mL of water to which has been added 1 mL of nitric acid, dilute with water to 1000 mL. Store in glass containers free from soluble lead salts.

Standard lead solution: On the day of use, dilute stock solution to suitable concentrations within the linear working range.

Analysis: Proceed according to 〈233〉.

Moisture Adsorption Capacity — General Test Method

Equipment: Temperature-humidity chambers capable of controlled humidity at 40% ± 5% RH and 80% ± 5% RH at 25 ± 2°, or a desiccator containing water-saturated salts that provide the required %RH, plus an oven capable of maintaining 25 ± 2°.

Method: 5–10 g. Remove the sample from the packaging material. Where absorbents are incorporated directly into the wall or cap of packaging containers, use unincorporated desiccant. Add the sample to the humidity chamber or desiccator and measure the weight gain over time until this reaches equilibrium when two successive consecutive weighings do not differ by more than 3 mg/g of substance taken, the second weighing following an additional 3 ± 1 h of storage at the required temperature and humidity conditions.

Calculate the adsorption capacity as percentage weight gained over the initial sample weight.

"Where two absorbents are packaged in combination, the moisture adsorption capacity specification must be calculated in proportion of the mix."

Calcium Chloride, Anhydrous —

Original Text

Calcium Chloride, Anhydrous

Specifications Summary:

  • Identification—calcium: Passes tests
  • Identification—chloride: Passes tests
  • Assay: NLT 93.0% and NMT 100.5% of calcium chloride (CaCl₂)
  • Arsenic: NMT 3 ppm
  • Fluoride: NMT 0.004%
  • Lead: NMT 5 ppm
  • Magnesium and alkali salts: NMT 25 mg of residue (NMT 5.0%)
  • pH 〈791〉: 4.5–11.0 (1:20 aqueous solution)
  • Moisture adsorption capacity: NLT 28% at 80% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°

Identification — Calcium: Insoluble oxalate salts are formed when solutions of calcium salts are treated: neutralize with 6 N ammonia, add 2.7 N HCl until acid. A white precipitate of calcium oxalate forms upon addition of ammonium oxalate TS — insoluble in acetic acid but dissolves in hydrochloric acid.

Identification — Chloride: Solutions of chlorides yield with silver nitrate TS a white, curdy precipitate that is insoluble in nitric acid but soluble in a slight excess of 6 N ammonia.

Assay (EDTA Titration):

Sample: 1.5 g. Transfer into a 250-mL volumetric flask, dissolve in 100 mL water + 5 mL of 2.7 N HCl, dilute to volume. Transfer 50 mL into suitable container, add 50 mL water. While stirring, add ~30 mL of 0.05 M disodium EDTA from a 50-mL buret. Add 15 mL of 1 N NaOH and 300 mg of hydroxy naphthol blue indicator. Continue titration to a blue endpoint.

"Each milliliter of 0.05 M disodium EDTA is equivalent to 5.55 mg of calcium chloride (CaCl₂)."

Fluoride — Ion-Selective Electrode Method:

Sodium fluoride solution (5 µg/mL): Transfer 2.210 g of sodium fluoride (dried at 200° for 4 h) into a 400-mL plastic beaker, add 200 mL water, stir until dissolved. Quantitatively transfer to 1000-mL volumetric flask, dilute to volume. Store in plastic bottle. On day of use, transfer 5.0 mL to 1000-mL flask, dilute to volume.

Calibration curve: Transfer 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, and 15.0 mL of sodium fluoride solution into separate 250-mL plastic beakers. Add 50 mL water, 5 mL of 1 N HCl, 10 mL of 1 M sodium citrate, and 10 mL of 0.2 M disodium EDTA to each beaker and mix. Transfer each to 100-mL volumetric flask, dilute to volume. Measure potential of 50-mL portions with fluoride ion-selective electrode. Plot on two-cycle semi-logarithmic paper or with a calculator/spreadsheet.

Analysis: Transfer 1.00 g sample into 150-mL glass beaker, add 10 mL water, slowly add 20 mL of 1 N HCl while stirring. Boil rapidly for 1 min, transfer to 250-mL plastic beaker, cool in ice water. Add 15 mL of 1 M sodium citrate and 10 mL of 0.2 M disodium EDTA, adjust pH to 5.5 ± 0.1. Transfer to 100-mL volumetric flask, dilute to volume. Measure 50-mL portion with fluoride ISE.

Calcium Oxide & Carbon, Activated —

Original Text

Calcium Oxide

Specifications Summary:

  • Identification—calcium: Passes tests
  • Assay: NLT 95.0% and NMT 100.5% of calcium oxide (CaO), on the ignited basis
  • Acid-insoluble substances: NMT 1%
  • Arsenic: NMT 3 ppm
  • Fluoride: NMT 0.015%
  • Lead: NMT 2 mg/kg
  • Magnesium and alkali salts: NMT 3.6%
  • Loss on ignition: NMT 10.0%
  • Moisture adsorption capacity: NLT 28% at 80% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°

Assay (Calcium Oxide):

Sample: 1 g of sample ignited to constant weight (see Loss on ignition below).

Analysis: Dissolve sample in 20 mL of 2.7 N HCl. Cool, dilute with water to 500.0 mL. Pipet 50.0 mL, add 50 mL water. Titrate with 0.05 M disodium EDTA (adding ~30 mL), then add 15 mL of 1 N NaOH and 300 mg hydroxy naphthol blue. Continue titration to blue endpoint.

"Each milliliter of 0.05 M disodium EDTA is equivalent to 2.804 mg of calcium oxide (CaO)."

Loss on ignition:

Sample: 1 g. Ignite the Sample to constant weight in a tared platinum crucible at 1100 ± 50°.

Acid-insoluble substances: Shake 5 g sample with 100 mL water and sufficient HCl to dissolve it. Boil, cool, filter through tared glass filter crucible. Wash with water, dry at 105° for 1 h, weigh.

Magnesium and alkali salts: Dissolve 500 mg in 30 mL water + 15 mL of 2.7 N HCl. Boil, add 40 mL oxalic acid TS, stir. Add methyl red TS, neutralize with 6 N ammonium hydroxide to precipitate calcium. Heat on steam bath for 1 h, cool. Dilute to 100 mL, filter. Add 0.5 mL H₂SO₄ to 50 mL filtrate, evaporate to dryness, ignite to constant weight in platinum crucible at 800 ± 25°.

Carbon, Activated (as Desiccant)

Carbon, activated (also known as "active carbon") is a solid, porous material obtained from carbonaceous source materials (vegetable matter) by suitable carbonization processes, treated to increase its adsorptive power.

Appearance: Black-colored powder or granule, or a mixture of powder and granule

Solubility: Insoluble in water and in organic solvents

Identification: When heated to redness in atmosphere, it burns slowly without a flame.

Specifications:

  • Arsenic: NMT 10 mg/kg (ppm)
  • Lead: NMT 15 mg/kg (ppm)
  • Loss on Drying 〈731〉: NMT 5% (5–10 g at 105°–120° to constant weight)
  • Residue on Ignition 〈281〉: NMT 5.0% (1.0 g sample)
  • Moisture adsorption capacity: NLT 21% at 80% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°
  • Higher aromatic hydrocarbons: No more color or fluorescence than Control solution under UV at 365 nm
  • Water extractables: NMT 5.0%

Inorganic impurities — Sample prep: 2.0 g + 50 mL dilute HCl, boil under reflux for 1 h. Filter, wash with dilute HCl, evaporate combined filtrate/washings to dryness. Dissolve residue in 0.1 M HCl, dilute to 50 mL. Proceed as directed under Bentonite for As and Pb.

Higher aromatic hydrocarbons:

Analysis: In a continuous-extraction apparatus, treat 1.0 g sample with 12 mL of cyclohexane for 2 h. Collect liquid, dilute with cyclohexane to 12 mL. Place in Nessler tube.

Control solution: 83 ng/mL of quinine in 0.1 N sulfuric acid in matching Nessler tube.

Water extractables:

Analysis: Transfer ~5 g (accurately weighed) to 250-mL flask with reflux condenser and Bunsen valve. Add 100 mL water and glass beads, reflux for 1 h. Cool, filter through filter paper, discard first 10 mL of filtrate. Pipet 25.0 mL into tared dish. Evaporate to incipient dryness on hot plate (never allow to boil). Dry for 1 h at 100° in a vacuum oven, cool, and weigh.

Calculate: percentage of water extractables based on sample weight and volume taken.

Molecular Sieves

Molecular sieves are synthetic porous crystalline alkali-metal aluminosilicates in a beaded form with a tightly controlled pore size. The most commonly used molecular sieves for desiccants are described as types 3A, 4A, 5A, and 13X with a pore size range of approximately 3–10 Å.

Identification A: The drop of water becomes turbid. [Full method: 500 mg + 2.5 g anhydrous K₂CO₃, heat in Pt/Ni crucible until melted. Cool, add 5 mL water, stand 3 min. Add HCl until no effervescence, add 10 mL more HCl, evaporate to dryness on steam bath. Cool, add 20 mL water, boil, filter through ash-free filter paper — insoluble residue of silica remains. Retain filtrate for Identification B. Treat gelatinous residue with 5 mL hydrofluoric acid (fume hood). The precipitate dissolves.]

Identification B — Aluminum: Two portions of filtrate from Identification A. First portion + 6 N ammonia → white, gelatinous precipitate insoluble in excess. Second portion + 1 N NaOH → white precipitate dissolved in excess. [Confirms amphoteric aluminum hydroxide.]

Lead: NMT 5 ppm

pH 〈791〉: 6.5–12 (200 mg/mL in CO₂-free water)

Loss on Drying 〈731〉: NMT 4.5% (5–10 g at 575 ± 25° to constant weight) [Note: conduct immediately after opening]

Moisture adsorption capacity:

  • NLT 15.0% at 40% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°
  • NLT 16.5% at 80% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°

Lead — Sample solution: Transfer 10.0 g sample into 250-mL beaker, add 50 mL of 0.5 N HCl, heat slowly to boiling. Boil for 15 min, cool, let undissolved material settle. Decant through filter paper into 100-mL volumetric flask, wash slurry with three 10-mL portions hot water, wash filter with 15 mL hot water. Cool filtrate, dilute to volume. Proceed as directed under Bentonite.

Silica Gel

Silica gel is silicon dioxide (SiO₂·H₂O) that has been manufactured by the addition of sodium silicate solution to a mineral acid to produce a gelatinous precipitate that is washed, then dehydrated to produce colorless silica gel in a bead, granular, or micronized form in a range of mesh sizes.

Appearance: A white or translucent bead or granule

Identification: A deep yellow color is produced. [Sample: 5 mg in Pt crucible, mix with 200 mg anhydrous K₂CO₃, ignite at red heat ~10 min. Cool, dissolve in 2 mL freshly distilled water, slowly add 2 mL ammonium molybdate TS → deep yellow color (silicomolybdic acid complex).]

Assay: NLT 94.0% of silicon dioxide (SiO₂) on the ignited basis

Lead: NMT 5 ppm

Soluble ionizable salts (as Na₂SO₄): Conductance NMT that of control solution (NMT 5.0%)

pH 〈791〉: 4–8 in slurry (1 in 20)

Loss on Drying 〈731〉: NMT 3.0% (5–10 g at 145° for 3 h)

Moisture adsorption capacity:

  • NLT 19% at 40% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°
  • NLT 27% at 80% ± 5% RH and 25 ± 2°

Silica Gel Assay (HF Dissolution):

Sample: 1 g, previously dried. Transfer to tared Pt crucible, ignite at 1000 ± 25° to constant weight; record ignited weight (W₁). Moisten residue with alcohol, add 3 drops H₂SO₄, add hydrofluoric acid to cover. [Caution — fume hood.] Evaporate to dryness at medium heat (95°–105°). Add more HF, swirl, evaporate to dryness again. Heat to red heat with Meker burner or muffle furnace. Ignite at 1000 ± 25° for 30 min, record final weight (W₂). Repeat until constant. The difference (W₁ − W₂) = weight of SiO₂.

Soluble ionizable salts — Analysis: Stir 5 g (previously dried) with 150 mL water for ≥5 min at >1500 rpm. Filter with suction, wash with 100 mL water, dilute filtrate to 250 mL. Determine conductances of filtrate and Control (1 mg/mL anhydrous Na₂SO₄ to 250 mL) with conductance bridge.
