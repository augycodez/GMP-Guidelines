Clause 1–4: Scope, Definitions, and Quality System Clause 1–4：、

Original Text

1 Scope

This document specifies requirements for sterilizing filtration as part of aseptic processing of health care products conducted in accordance with ISO 13408-1. It also offers guidance to filter users concerning general requirements for set-up, validation and routine operation of a sterilizing filtration process.

This document is not applicable to removal of viruses. Sterilizing filtration is not applicable to fluids that intentionally contain particles larger than the pore size of the filter (e.g. bacterial whole-cell vaccines).

Introduction — Key Distinctions

"During terminal sterilization the kinetics of inactivation follows a mathematical order and allow calculation of a sterility assurance level (SAL). Removal of organisms from a fluid by filtration does not follow such mathematical order and so the use of the term 'sterility assurance level' is not appropriate for product sterilized by filtration."

Key Definitions

  • Sterilizing filtration (3.11): Removal of viable microorganisms from fluids by passage through a filter under specified process conditions resulting in a sterile filtrate
  • Bacterial challenge test (3.1): Technical operation to evaluate the capability of a filter to retain organisms from liquid bacterial suspension under defined conditions
  • Filter integrity test (3.7): Non-destructive physical test that can be correlated to the bacterial retention capability of a filter assembly
  • Extractable (3.4): Substance that can be released from a filter using extraction solvents or conditions expected to be at least as aggressive as normal use conditions
  • Leachable (3.8): Substance that can be released from a filter or filter assembly during normal use conditions

4.3 Procurement of Filters

4.3.2 There shall be a written agreement between the filter user and filter manufacturer that the filter manufacturer will notify the filter user of any changes in the filter manufacturing conditions with potential to affect the defined fluid and process parameters.

Clauses 5–9: Filter Characterization, Process Definition, and Validation Clauses 5–9：、

Original Text

5.2 Microbial Removal Effectiveness

5.2.1 Microbial removal effectiveness data shall be developed for each combination of sterilizing grade filter and fluid type. This is usually by demonstration of retention of a product-specific microbial challenge for liquid filtration.

5.2.2 Variables that affect microbial removal effectiveness include:

  • Filter membrane characteristics (pore size distribution, surface chemistry, polymer type)
  • Fluid characteristics (surfactants, pH, viscosity, osmolarity, surface tension, ionic strength)
  • Fluid bioburden (number, type and cell size of organisms)
  • Process conditions (batch size, temperature, differential pressure, flow rate, hold times)

8.4 Integrity Testing Process Definition

8.4.1 A sterilizing grade filter shall be subject to a non-destructive integrity test post use and without removal of the filter from its housing. Test results shall correlate to the microbial retention capability.

8.4.2 The integrity of the filtration system prior to use is critical to ensure product sterility. Consideration should be given to carrying out integrity testing pre- and post-filtration (PUPSIT). The decision to omit the pre-use test shall be based on risk assessment and documented.

9.2 Validation of Microbial Retention

9.2.1.1 Liquid-sterilizing filtration shall be validated during initial process qualification by an appropriate bacterial challenge test using at least one filter from each of not less than three lots of filters with three consecutive successful outcomes.

9.2.2.2 If the fluid does not contain organisms smaller than Brevundimonas diminuta, the challenge organism for a 0.2 µm sterilizing filter shall be Brevundimonas diminuta (ATCC 19146).

9.2.2.5 The minimum challenge level shall be 1 × 10⁷ CFU/cm² of effective filter surface area.

9.2.2.6 Where a 0.1 µm sterilizing filter is selected to prevent the passage of Mycoplasma or other organisms smaller than B. diminuta, the challenge organism may be Acholeplasma laidlawii (ATCC 23206). The minimum challenge level shall be 1 × 10⁷ CFU/cm².

Clauses 10–12: Routine Monitoring, Product Release, and Process Maintenance Clauses 10–12：、

Original Text

10 Routine Monitoring and Control

10.1 A programme for the routine monitoring and control of the sterilizing filtration process shall be established to confirm that the process continues to operate within the validated process parameters.

10.2 Each production filtration shall be traceable to the specific filtration equipment, filter lot, equipment qualification status, filter condition before and after use and the environmental data recorded during filtration.

10.3 Process variables that shall be monitored during routine production filtration include:

  • temperature of the fluid during filtration
  • differential pressure across the filter
  • flow rate and/or total volume
  • filtration time

11 Product Release from Sterilizing Filtration

11.1 The filtration process shall be considered complete when:

  • a) all process parameters are within validated limits
  • b) the integrity test of the sterilizing filter has been performed and passed

11.2 If a post-use filter integrity test fails, the batch shall be rejected unless it can be demonstrated that the failure was not caused by inadequate microbial removal by the sterilizing filter. Any re-filtration shall be evaluated for its potential impact on product quality.

12 Maintaining Process Effectiveness

12.4 Requalification of the filtration process shall be considered following:

  • significant changes to the process fluid (formulation, concentration, pH, viscosity)
  • significant changes to the filtration equipment
  • changes to the filter itself (different lot characteristics, manufacturer's notification)
  • changes to the sterilization procedure
  • failures identified during routine integrity testing

12.5 Assessment of Change: If there is a change in the fluid to be filtered (e.g. changes in raw materials, chemical composition, concentrations, viscosity, changes to processing conditions), it is necessary to assess the change to see if it could have an adverse effect on the performance of the sterilizing grade filter, or whether it might invalidate the existing filter validation studies.
