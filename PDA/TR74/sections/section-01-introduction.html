1.0 Introduction

Original Text

Reprocessing and reworking are tools that biological manufacturers can use when a manufacturing process step has operated outside of its validated operating ranges. For reprocessing, rather than proceeding with the next manufacturing process step, the product is rerun through the previous manufacturing process step operated within the acceptable operating ranges. For reworking, the product is subjected to one or more processing steps that are different from the established manufacturing process to obtain acceptable product.

In biological manufacturing, however, reprocessing, and especially reworking, should be considered only in exceptional circumstances. Reworking can be considered during events such as critical equipment failures, out of specifications of critical quality attributes of drug substance or drug product, and/or deviation from in-process controls that impact quality of the drug substance or drug product (further conditions to consider are stated in Section 5.0).

Reprocessing and reworking are deviations that are managed within the corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) of the manufacturer's quality management system. An essential prerequisite for the acceptance of a reprocessing or reworking step is a clear indication of the root cause. Due to the size and complexity of biological products, including the limitations in testing methods (especially measurement of functional biological activity, i.e., potency), reprocessing, and especially reworking, are limited for biologicals. Thus, they should be entered into with caution so as not to impact the product's critical quality attributes (CQA), and the costs and risks should be carefully evaluated and documented.

Effective reprocessing and reworking strategies should integrate supply chain resilience considerations, including forecasting, inventory buffers, and manufacturing scheduling. Combining quality risk management assessments (e.g., a FMEA assessment) with supply risk scoring can support balanced decisions that protect both compliance and patient access.

Reprocessing and reworking also require regulatory authority approval before the manufactured product can be released for human use. In limited cases, regulatory authority approval for reprocessing can be acquired ahead of time by presenting a comprehensive change management plan (proactive approach detailed in Section 3.6). This plan should explain the reason for reprocessing, such as refiltration or reconcentration due to technical failures or mechanical breakdown of a chromatography column (1). It must also specify the frequency and nature of the reprocessing procedures for potential events, as well as detail the steps for validation and ensuring product comparability.

1.1 Purpose

Original Text

PDA Technical Report No. 74 (Revised 2026): Reprocessing and Reworking of Biologicals (TR 74) presents a strategy based on the experiences and practices of a PDA Technical Report Team representing a cross-section of industry professionals. The intent is to provide guidance on the design, development, controls, procedures, validation, regulatory submission, and implementation of reprocessing and reworking procedures for the manufacturing of biologicals.

The information discussed represents what the authors consider a balance of scientific, regulatory, and business considerations for the reprocessing of biologicals in particular but can also be applied to the reworking of biologicals in general. It is highly recommended that manufacturers consult with the appropriate regulatory authority for agreement on the strategies employed for any biological reprocessing or reworking operation.

1.2 Scope

Original Text

The previous version of TR 74 included only such biopharmaceutical products as recombinant proteins and polypeptides. Because the principles of reprocessing and reworking are applicable across a wider array of manufacturing processes, the scope has been expanded to include guidance on biological products. In addition to recombinant proteins and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) (both as innovators/novel and biosimilars), biological products (2–4) encompass a wide range of other products such as vaccines and blood-derived proteins.

The quality of biologicals is defined by the chosen production and manufacturing process. Minor changes in the process can affect the quality of the drug product; therefore, the development of the manufacturing process is of paramount importance for biologicals, including the implementation of any reprocessing or reworking. Incorporating the topic of reworking further expands the scope of this technical report, but because the reworking of biologicals is far more challenging than reprocessing, the authors emphasize that extra caution should be taken.

The guidance in this technical report is general in nature, and the case studies illustrate how the general principles may be applied. No attempt is made to encompass all types of unit operations for which reprocessing or reworking could be considered.

This technical report is not intended to establish mandatory standards but serves as a single-source overview that complements existing guidance documents listed in Section 8.0.

The principles discussed in TR 74 are applicable to both clinical and commercial manufacturing processes, but the authors recognize that reprocessing and reworking during the clinical manufacturing of biologicals is often more challenging than during commercial manufacturing. This is primarily because the understanding of the product and the process is very limited, so extra caution is emphasized.
