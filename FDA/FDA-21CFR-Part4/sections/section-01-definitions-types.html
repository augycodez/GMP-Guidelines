1.1 §4.1 — What Does This Part Cover?（Part 4 ）

Original Text

§4.1 — Scope.

(a) This subpart applies to combination products as defined in §3.2(e) of this chapter. It establishes, for purposes of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and the Public Health Service Act, which current good manufacturing practice (CGMP) requirements in parts 4, 210, 211, 600 through 680, and 820 of this chapter apply to combination products, and provides for the assignment of combination products to a component of FDA.

(b) This subpart does not cover biological products that are regulated solely under the Public Health Service Act and that do not have a drug or device constituent part.

(c) Agreements and decisions made under this part regarding which CGMP requirements apply to a specific combination product are intended to promote the efficient and timely review of applications and the timely compliance of manufacturers with applicable CGMP requirements.

"This subpart applies to combination products as defined in §3.2(e) of this chapter."
— 21 CFR §4.1(a)

1.2 §4.2 — Key Definitions（）

Original Text

§4.2 — Definitions.

The following definitions apply to this subpart:

Combination product means a product comprised of any combination of a drug and a device; a biological product and a device; a drug and a biological product; or a drug, a device, and a biological product. A combination product includes: (1) A product comprised of two or more regulated components that are physically, chemically, or otherwise combined or mixed and produced as a single entity (single-entity combination product); (2) two or more separate products packaged together in a single package or as a unit and comprised of drug and device products, device and biological products, or biological and drug products (co-packaged combination product); (3) a drug, device, or biological product packaged separately that according to its investigational plan or proposed labeling is intended for use only with an approved individually specified drug, device, or biological product where both are required to achieve the intended use, indication, or effect and where upon approval of the proposed product the labeling of the approved product would need to be changed, e.g., to reflect a change in intended use, dosage form, strength, route of administration, or significant change in dose (cross-labeled combination product); (4) any investigational drug, device, or biological product packaged separately that according to its proposed labeling is for use only with another individually specified investigational drug, device, or biological product where both are required to achieve the intended use, indication, or effect. (See §3.2(e) of this chapter.)

Constituent part means a drug, device, or biological product that is part of a combination product.

Primary mode of action (PMOA) means the single mode of action of a combination product that provides the most important therapeutic action of the combination product. The most important therapeutic action is the mode of action expected to make the greatest contribution to the overall intended therapeutic effects of the combination product.

Lead center (also referred to as designated center) means the center within FDA that has primary jurisdiction for the premarket review and regulation of a combination product, as determined under §3.4 of this chapter.

Requesting party means any person who submits a request for designation (RFD) under §3.7 of this chapter or an agreement/determination request under this part.

Office of Combination Products (OCP) means the office within the Office of the Commissioner that administers the combination product designation provisions of section 503(g) of the act.

1.3 §4.3 — Which CGMP Regulations Apply?（ CGMP ）

Original Text

§4.3 — Applicable CGMP requirements.

(a) Combination products with a drug constituent part. The CGMP requirements of parts 210 and 211 of this chapter apply to combination products that include a drug constituent part.

(b) Combination products with a device constituent part. The CGMP requirements of part 820 of this chapter (quality system regulation) apply to combination products that include a device constituent part.

(c) Combination products with a biological product constituent part. The CGMP requirements of parts 600 through 680 of this chapter apply to combination products that include a biological product constituent part.

(d) Combination products with an HCT/P constituent part. The requirements of part 1271 of this chapter apply to combination products that include a human cell, tissue, or cellular or tissue-based product (HCT/P) constituent part.

(e) Single-entity and co-packaged combination products. For single-entity or co-packaged combination products, manufacturers must comply with the CGMP requirements applicable to each constituent part. The combination product is subject to all of those requirements unless a streamlined approach is agreed upon under §4.4.

Note on Streamlined Compliance (§4.4): A manufacturer of a single-entity or co-packaged combination product containing a device and either a drug or biological product may petition the lead center to demonstrate compliance using the primary constituent part's CGMP framework, provided the manufacturer can show that the other applicable CGMP requirements are also satisfied through that framework.

"For single-entity or co-packaged combination products, manufacturers must comply with the CGMP requirements applicable to each constituent part."
— 21 CFR §4.3(e)

Cross-labeled combination products: Cross-labeled combination products are subject to the CGMP requirements applicable to their individual constituent parts as regulated independently. Each constituent part must comply with its own applicable CGMP regime.
